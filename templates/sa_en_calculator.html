Compare gold bar offers

A tool for comparing bullion offers. Bars are sold by fineness in parts per thousand (999 or 999.9), not by karat. This sets the offer you have beside the reference metal value at the market price. It does not pick a dealer and does not say when to buy.

Fineness is parts per thousand: 999.9 means 99.99% gold, 999 means 99.9%. We do not treat "24K" as an exact synonym for 999.9, and we never apply a karat conversion and a fineness conversion together.

Dealer offers (optional)

Enter the total you would pay for the selected bar, including any charges the dealer adds — not a per-gram quote. Compare both offers on the same weight and the same fineness, and ask each dealer exactly what their number includes.

The comparison here is against the reference metal value only. A difference from it may cover fabrication, the dealer's margin and other charges, and we do not know how that difference is made up. A total below the reference value is not proof of a bargain and not evidence of wrongdoing. We also do not assert that the amount you entered includes, or is exempt from, any particular tax — ask the dealer. And things other than the number matter first: the dealer's reliability, the bar's authenticity and assay certificate, delivery and storage, and the terms on which you could sell it back.

How it works

Value = weight × today's per-gram price for that karat. A making charge, if you enter one, is added per gram to approximate what a shop would charge. The making charge is a number you ask the shop for — we do not know it and do not estimate it.
